To simplify this design in CAD, you would replace the multi-chip power management network (BQ25570 and TPS61022 boost converter) and tiny surface-mount components with a fundamental, 5-component energy-harvesting loop.

### Key Simplifications

1. **Single Input & Pre-Packaged Rectifier**
* **Original Design:** Three separate piezoelectric inputs requiring 12 individual surface-mount Schottky diodes to form three discrete bridges.


* **Simpler Version:** A single piezoelectric input feeding into **one pre-packaged bridge rectifier chip** (such as an HD01 or MB10S in a DIP package) or four basic through-hole diodes (e.g., 1N5817).


2. **Streamlined Energy Storage (No Tuning Resistor Networks)**
* **Original Design:** The BQ25570 IC requires complex MPPT configuration, cold-start inductors, and seven high-precision resistor dividers to set voltage thresholds.


* **Simpler Version (Passive):** Connect the rectified DC directly to a **smoothing capacitor** (e.g., 100 $\mu$F) and a **5.1 V Zener diode** to clamp safe voltage levels, charging a supercapacitor or rechargeable battery directly.
* **Simpler Version (Active IC Alternative):** Use an all-in-one chip like the **LTC3588-1**, which integrates the bridge rectifiers and high-efficiency buck regulator specifically for piezoelectric elements onto a single chip with minimal external components.


3. **Direct Output (Eliminating the USB Boost Stage)**
* **Original Design:** A TPS61022 boost converter, feedback network, user switch, and custom-bridged USB data lines to step battery voltage up to 5 V for phone charging.


* **Simpler Version:** Omit the USB boost stage entirely. Output the energy directly to a 2-pin screw terminal block, test points, or a low-current indicator LED.




4. **Through-Hole Technology (THT) vs. Surface Mount (SMD)**
* **Original Design:** Small SMD footprints, fine-pitch QFN chip packages, and roughly 100 ground stitching vias.


* **Simpler Version:** Use Through-Hole (THT) components (axial resistors, DIP chips, radial capacitors). THT footprints in KiCad have large drill holes and generous copper pad clearances, allowing you to route traces manually on a simple 2-layer board without needing autorouters or complex Design Rule Checks (DRC).

### The Simplest Schematic Chain in CAD

$$\text{Piezo Header} \longrightarrow \text{Bridge Rectifier} \longrightarrow \text{Storage Cap} \longrightarrow \text{Zener Diode Clamp} \longrightarrow \text{Output Terminal / LED}$$

This approach drops the total component count from roughly 40 components down to 5 or 6, fitting the entire CAD schematic onto a single clean sheet and allowing the PCB traces to be routed manually in a few minutes.
